### 3  |  The 360-degree colour ruler & grid geometry

Think of the South-Indian chart as a *4 × 4 matrix* whose outside ring (12 squares) holds the rāśis, while the inner 2 × 2 block is merged to display birth data.
Index the cells as **row × column** from **0 to 3**:

```
(0,0) (0,1) (0,2) (0,3)
(1,0) ┌─── central ───┐ (1,3)
(2,0) └───   block  ──┘ (2,3)
(3,0) (3,1) (3,2) (3,3)
```

| Matrix cell | Sign (fixed)  |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| (0,0)       | **Pisces**    |
| (0,1)       | **Aries**     |
| (0,2)       | Taurus        |
| (0,3)       | Gemini        |
| (1,3)       | **Cancer**    |
| (2,3)       | Leo           |
| (3,3)       | Virgo         |
| (3,2)       | **Libra**     |
| (3,1)       | Scorpio       |
| (3,0)       | Sagittarius   |
| (2,0)       | **Capricorn** |
| (1,0)       | Aquarius      |

The rainbow-coloured ruler that frames the chart is a *one-degree‐per‐pixel* circle laid on top of this square.  Its four anchor points align with sign cusps exactly at the corners of certain cells:

| Ecliptic degree | Geometric reference                   | Zodiac cusp  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **0°**          | **Top-left corner of cell (0,1)**     | 0° Aries     |
| **90°**         | **Top-right corner of cell (1,3)**    | 0° Cancer    |
| **180°**        | **Bottom-right corner of cell (3,2)** | 0° Libra     |
| **270°**        | **Bottom-left corner of cell (2,0)**  | 0° Capricorn |

So when you glance at the colour bar:

* any tick **between 0° and 90°** must sit somewhere along the *top edge* of the matrix (signs Aries → Taurus → Gemini);
* **90°–180°** runs down the *right edge* (Cancer → Leo → Virgo);
* **180°–270°** spans the *bottom edge* (Libra → Scorpio → Sagittarius);
* **270°–360°/0°** climbs the *left edge* (Capricorn → Aquarius → Pisces).

Planetary tick-marks pierce that outer ring at their exact sidereal longitudes, letting you see hemispheric clusters instantly while the fixed-sign grid keeps orientation unambiguous.

### 4  | Added detail — the *pada ruler* that hugs the outside of the square

The rainbow-like strip is **more than decoration**: it is a 360-degree micrometer that lets you read off *nakṣatra padas* (quarters of lunar mansions) at a glance.

| Visual cue                           | What it represents              | Why 3 ° 20′?                                                       |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Tiny notch every 3 ° 20′ ≈ 3.33°** | One **pada** (¼ of a nakṣatra). | 27 nakṣatras × 4 padas = **108** divisions ⇒ 360° ÷ 108 ≈ 3 ° 20′. |

#### How the colour rhythm is laid out

* **Group-of-4 rule:** the ruler is painted in blocks of **4 consecutive padas**, i.e. one full nakṣatra (13 ° 20′).
* **Colour cycle:** *each* four-pada block is given a colour that is **different from the block immediately before it**.

  * In practice you can rotate through 5-to-7 pastel hues so that neighbouring nakṣatras never share the same shade; after a few signs the palette repeats, keeping the band readable yet visually lively.
* **Tick hierarchy:**

  * *Minor ticks* (thin) mark **every** 3 ° 20′.
  * *Medium ticks* (slightly longer) emphasise the start of each coloured block—i.e., **0° of a nakṣatra**.
  * *Major ticks* (longest + black) flag **sign cusps** every 30°, coinciding with the four anchor corners you mapped earlier:

    * 0° Aries at the *top-left* of cell (0, 1)
    * 0° Cancer at the *top-right* of cell (1, 3)
    * 0° Libra at the *bottom-right* of cell (3, 2)
    * 0° Capricorn at the *bottom-left* of cell (2, 0)

#### Reading planets against the ruler

1. **Locate the planet’s tick**—each planet gets a thin black hair-line that pierces the ruler at its exact sidereal longitude.
2. **Note the colour block:** that tells you instantly which **nakṣatra** the planet occupies (because every block = one nakṣatra).
3. **Count the minor ticks** from the block start (0, 1, 2, 3) to see which **pada** (1-to-4) it falls in.
4. Combine with the fixed-grid sign to know both *rāśi* and *nakṣatra-pada* without ever reading numbers.

#### Quick mental example

*Suppose Saturn’s hair-line sits in a pale-blue block and is the third minor tick in that block:*

* Pale-blue block might be **Hasta** (just an example).
* Third tick → **3rd pada** of Hasta.
* From the square grid you already know Saturn is in Virgo. Result: *Saturn in Hasta-3, Virgo sign.*

With this colour-coded pada ruler, the eye learns to **decode sign + nakṣatra + pada** in one sweep, while the major ticks keep your 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° orientation rock-solid.
